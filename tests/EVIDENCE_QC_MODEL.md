# 1. Introduction

This document defines a structured Quality-Control (QC) model for
organizing and reviewing preclinical and biosimilarity-related evidence.

The purpose of this model is to identify whether an evidence record
contains sufficient information for structured scientific review.

The QC model focuses on:

- Evidence traceability
- Study documentation
- Sample identification
- Control identification
- Measurement methods
- Method validation
- Raw-data availability
- Missing information
- Protocol deviations
- Statistical reporting
- Reproducibility
- Reviewer assessment
- Evidence limitations
- Final review status

This model is intended for scientific learning and evidence
organization.

It is **not** a regulatory approval system.

# 2. Objective

The objective of the QC model is to provide a consistent framework for
checking the completeness and quality of evidence records.

The workflow is:

```text
Evidence Record
      ↓
Record Identification
      ↓
Source Verification
      ↓
Study Information Check
      ↓
Sample / Control Check
      ↓
Measurement Check
      ↓
Data Availability Check
      ↓
Deviation Check
      ↓
Statistical Check
      ↓
Reproducibility Check
      ↓
Limitation Review
      ↓
Human Review
      ↓
Final QC Status
. Scope
This QC model can be applied conceptually to records related to:
- Analytical evidence
- Structural evidence
- Functional evidence
- Pharmacokinetic evidence
- Pharmacodynamic evidence
- Immunogenicity
- Preclinical evidence
- Animal-study evidence
- Clinical evidence
The primary focus of this document is preclinical and non-clinical
evidence.
4. Important Scientific Principle
Evidence quality and scientific conclusions are different concepts.
For example:
Evidence Quality = VERIFIED
does NOT mean:
Candidate = Biosimilar
Similarly:
Evidence Quality = INSUFFICIENT
does NOT automatically mean:
Candidate = Unsafe
A QC status describes the condition of the available evidence record.
Scientific conclusions require appropriate scientific interpretation and
human review.

6. Detailed QC Fields
6.1 Study ID
Purpose
The Study ID provides a unique identifier for the study or evidence
record.
QC Check
Confirm that:
- A Study ID exists.
- The Study ID is unique.
- The identifier is consistent across records.
- The identifier can be traced to the source.
Example
STUDY-001
Possible Problem
Study ID missing
QC Status
NEEDS REVIEW
7. Source
Purpose
The Source field identifies where the evidence originated.
Possible sources include:
- Regulatory document
- Scientific article
- Study report
- Laboratory record
- Public database
- Synthetic dataset
QC Check
Verify:
- Source name
- Source type
- Source location
- Publication/reference information where applicable
- Whether the source can be traced
Important Rule
A synthetic record must be clearly identified as:
SAMPLE / SYNTHETIC / FOR SOFTWARE TESTING
Synthetic evidence must never be presented as real experimental evidence.
8. Protocol / Reference
Purpose
This field identifies the protocol, study reference, guideline, or other
document associated with the evidence.
QC Check
Determine whether:
- The protocol/reference is identified.
- The reference is traceable.
- The evidence can be understood in relation to the stated protocol or
  reference.
Possible Problems
- Protocol unavailable
- Reference unclear
- Incomplete study documentation
These conditions may require:
PARTIAL
or
NEEDS REVIEW
9. Sample Identification
Purpose
Sample identification provides traceability between the evidence and
the product or sample being evaluated.
QC Check
Check whether:
- Sample identity is documented.
- Candidate product is identified.
- Reference product is identified where applicable.
- Sample identifiers are consistent.
- Records can be traced.
Importance
Poor sample identification can reduce confidence in evidence
interpretation.
10. Control Identification
Purpose
Controls provide a comparison basis for interpreting study observations.
QC Check
Check whether:
- Control is identified.
- Control type is documented.
- Control is appropriate for the scientific question.
- Control information is traceable.
Important Principle
The presence of a control does not automatically prove that the study is
scientifically valid.
The appropriateness of the control must be considered.
11. Experimental Conditions
Purpose
Experimental conditions provide context for understanding the evidence.
Depending on the study, relevant information may include:
- Study conditions
- Product identity
- Relevant treatment information
- Observation period
- Study groups
- Measurement conditions
QC Check
Determine whether sufficient information is available to understand the
context of the evidence.
This project does not provide operational instructions for conducting
laboratory or animal experiments.
12. Measurement Method
Purpose
The measurement method identifies how the evidence was generated or
measured.
Examples include:
- Chromatographic method
- Mass spectrometry
- Functional assay
- Biomarker measurement
- PK assay
- PD measurement
- Immunogenicity assay
QC Check
Check whether:
- The method is identified.
- The method is appropriate for the measurement.
- The method status is documented.
- The measurement can be interpreted.
13. Instrument / Method Validation Status
Purpose
This field records whether the measurement method or instrument
information has been appropriately documented.
Possible statuses include:
VERIFIED
PARTIAL
NEEDS REVIEW
INSUFFICIENT
QC Considerations
Check for:
- Method validation information
- Method qualification where appropriate
- Instrument status
- Calibration information where applicable
- Assay performance
- Measurement reliability
Important Principle
A validated method does not automatically make every scientific
conclusion correct.
14. Raw-Data Availability
Purpose
Raw data support traceability and independent review.
QC Check
Record whether supporting raw data are:
AVAILABLE
PARTIAL
NOT AVAILABLE
UNKNOWN
Interpretation
If raw data are unavailable, the evidence may still be documented, but
the limitation should be recorded.
Do not assume missing raw data means that the underlying study was
invalid.
15. Missing Data
Purpose
The Missing Data field identifies information that is unavailable or
incomplete.
Examples:
- Missing measurements
- Missing sample information
- Missing control information
- Missing statistical information
- Missing study documentation
QC Check
Record:
- What is missing?
- How much information is missing?
- Could the missing information affect interpretation?
- Has the issue been reviewed?
16. Protocol Deviations
Purpose
Protocol deviations identify departures from the planned study
procedures.
QC Check
Determine:
- Whether deviations occurred.
- Whether they were documented.
- Whether their potential impact was assessed.
- Whether the reviewer considered them important.
Important Principle
A protocol deviation does not automatically invalidate a study.
The scientific significance of the deviation must be assessed.
17. Statistical Reporting
Purpose
Statistical reporting provides information about how study data were
analyzed and interpreted.
QC Check
Look for:
- Statistical method
- Relevant summary measures
- Variability
- Sample information
- Missing-data handling
- Appropriate comparisons
- Confidence intervals where applicable
- Clearly reported results
Important Principle
Statistical significance alone does not establish scientific
importance.
18. Reproducibility
Purpose
Reproducibility considers whether evidence can be consistently
observed under appropriate conditions.
QC Check
Review:
- Repeat measurements where available
- Consistency across relevant observations
- Method variability
- Study variability
- Documentation quality
Limitations
A single study may provide limited information about reproducibility.
Do not assume that one successful result proves complete
reproducibility.
19. Reviewer Status
Purpose
Reviewer status identifies the stage of human review.
Suggested values:
NOT REVIEWED
IN REVIEW
REVIEWED
REQUIRES SECOND REVIEW
CLOSED
Important Principle
The evidence tracker is intended to support human review.
It should not replace qualified scientific judgment.
20. Evidence Quality Statuses
The project uses five main evidence-quality statuses.
20.1 VERIFIED
Definition
The evidence record has been checked against the defined QC fields and
the available documentation is sufficiently complete for the intended
organization purpose.
Example
Evidence Quality: VERIFIED
Important Limitation
VERIFIED does not mean:
- Scientifically proven
- Clinically effective
- Clinically safe
- Biosimilar
- Regulatory approved
20.2 PARTIAL
Definition
The record contains useful information but some required information is
missing or incomplete.
Example
Evidence Quality: PARTIAL

Limitation:
Raw-data information is incomplete.
20.3 NEEDS REVIEW
Definition
The evidence record requires additional human review before the quality
can be confidently classified.
Example
Evidence Quality: NEEDS REVIEW

Reviewer Note:
Method validation status requires verification.
20.4 CONFLICTING
Definition
Different pieces of evidence or documentation appear inconsistent or
contradictory.
Example
Evidence Quality: CONFLICTING

Limitation:
Two source records report inconsistent findings.
Important Rule
Conflicting evidence must not automatically be resolved by the
software.
Human scientific review is required.
20.5 INSUFFICIENT
Definition
The available information is not sufficient to support reliable
evidence organization for the intended purpose.
Example
Evidence Quality: INSUFFICIENT

Limitation:
Study source and measurement method are unavailable.
21. Evidence Quality Decision Framework
A conceptual QC decision process is:
Is the record identifiable?
        |
        +---- NO → INSUFFICIENT / NEEDS REVIEW
        |
       YES
        ↓
Is the source traceable?
        |
        +---- NO → NEEDS REVIEW
        |
       YES
        ↓
Are samples and controls identified?
        |
        +---- NO → PARTIAL / NEEDS REVIEW
        |
       YES
        ↓
Is the measurement method documented?
        |
        +---- NO → PARTIAL / NEEDS REVIEW
        |
       YES
        ↓
Is supporting data available?
        |
        +---- NO → PARTIAL + LIMITATION
        |
       YES
        ↓
Are there unresolved conflicts?
        |
        +---- YES → CONFLICTING
        |
        NO
        ↓
Is the record sufficiently documented?
        |
        +---- YES → VERIFIED
        |
        +---- NO → PARTIAL / NEEDS REVIEW
This is an organizational decision framework, not a scientific
approval algorithm.

24. Quality-Control Rules
Rule 1 — No Fabricated Evidence
The QC model must never invent:
- Experimental results
- Clinical outcomes
- Animal-study results
- Laboratory measurements
- Regulatory conclusions
Rule 2 — Synthetic Data Must Be Identified
All synthetic records must contain:
SAMPLE / SYNTHETIC / FOR SOFTWARE TESTING
Rule 3 — Missing Information Must Be Visible
Missing information should be recorded rather than silently ignored.
Rule 4 — Limitations Must Be Recorded
Every evidence record should contain a limitation field.
If there are no known limitations, the record should state:
No limitation identified in the available record; human review required.
This avoids assuming that no limitation exists.
Rule 5 — Conflicts Must Be Preserved
Conflicting evidence should not be automatically deleted or averaged
away.
It should be flagged for human review.
Rule 6 — Quality Status Is Not Scientific Conclusion
The following mapping must NOT be used:
VERIFIED → Biosimilar
INSUFFICIENT → Unsafe
CONFLICTING → Ineffective
PARTIAL → Failed
The statuses describe evidence-record quality only.
25. Evidence Completeness
The tracker should identify incomplete evidence records.
A record may be considered incomplete if one or more important fields
are missing, such as:
- Evidence ID
- Reference product
- Candidate product
- Evidence category
- Study/source
- Measurement
- Control
- Evidence quality
- Validation status
- Limitation
Incomplete records should be flagged for review.
26. Evidence Conflict Handling
When conflicting evidence is identified:
1. Preserve both records.
2. Do not automatically delete one.
3. Identify the source of each record.
4. Document the conflict.
5. Mark the record as CONFLICTING where appropriate.
6. Record a reviewer note.
7. Request human scientific review.
Example:
Evidence Quality:
CONFLICTING

Limitation:
Two evidence sources report different observations.

Reviewer Note:
Source comparison required before interpretation.
27. Evidence Gap Identification
The QC model should help identify evidence gaps.
Examples include:
Missing analytical evidence
Missing functional evidence
Incomplete PK information
Incomplete PD information
Incomplete immunogenicity information
Missing preclinical documentation
Missing control information
Missing validation information
Incomplete statistical reporting
Evidence gaps should be reported as information gaps rather than
automatically interpreted as product failures.

33. Human Review Requirements
The QC model supports but does not replace human review.
Human review is especially important when:
- Evidence is conflicting.
- Important data are missing.
- Method validation is unclear.
- Protocol deviations may affect interpretation.
- Study conclusions appear stronger than the available evidence.
- The evidence may influence scientific or regulatory decisions.
The reviewer should record:
- Reviewer status
- Reviewer note
- Limitations
- Final review status
34. What This QC Model Does NOT Prove
This QC model does NOT prove:
- Biosimilarity
- Clinical equivalence
- Clinical efficacy
- Clinical safety
- Interchangeability
- Regulatory approval
- Manufacturing quality
- Long-term safety
- Suitability for a particular patient
It only provides a structured framework for organizing and reviewing
evidence quality.
35. Scientific Limitations
The QC model has several limitations.
1. It is an organizational framework
It does not replace detailed scientific evaluation.
2. It uses predefined fields
Some studies may require additional information.
3. Evidence quality is context-dependent
A record may be sufficient for one purpose but insufficient for another.
4. Human review is required
Automated classification cannot replace scientific judgment.
5. Regulatory requirements vary
Different jurisdictions may have different requirements.
6. Product-specific considerations matter
Biological products differ in structure, mechanism, and risk.

38. Key Learning Points
The main lessons from the QC model are:
1. Evidence must be traceable.
2. Study and sample identification are essential.
3. Controls provide a basis for comparison.
4. Measurement methods must be documented.
5. Method validation or qualification status should be recorded.
6. Raw-data availability affects evidence traceability.
7. Missing data should be explicitly documented.
8. Protocol deviations should be recorded and assessed.
9. Statistical reporting should be transparent.
10. Reproducibility contributes to confidence in evidence.
11. Data integrity is essential.
12. Conflicting evidence should be preserved and reviewed.
13. Evidence limitations must be visible.
14. Quality statuses should not be converted into scientific conclusions.
15. Human scientific review remains essential.
39. Final Statement
The Preclinical Evidence Quality-Control Model provides a structured
approach for checking the completeness, traceability, documentation,
and review status of biosimilarity and preclinical evidence.
The central principle is:
Good evidence organization
        +
Good documentation
        +
Quality control
        +
Transparent limitations
        +
Human scientific review
        =
More reliable evidence interpretation
However:
Evidence organization is not the same as scientific proof.

And:
This is an evidence-organisation tool, not a regulatory approval
system.
