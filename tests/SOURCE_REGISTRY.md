# 1. Purpose of This Source Registry

This document records the authoritative and scientific sources used for
the Biosimilarity & Preclinical Evidence Quality Foundation project.

The purpose of the registry is to make the research:

- Traceable
- Reproducible
- Easy to review
- Scientifically responsible
- Separated from unsupported assumptions
- Useful for future evidence-management work

The registry distinguishes between:

1. Regulatory guidance
2. International scientific guidance
3. Scientific literature
4. Experimental evidence

The sources listed here are used for learning and evidence organization.

They do not by themselves establish that any particular candidate
product is biosimilar.

---

# 2. Source Classification

| Source Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory guidance | Guidance or information issued by a regulatory authority | FDA biosimilar guidance |
| International guidance | Guidance developed by international organizations | WHO similar biotherapeutic product guidance |
| Scientific guideline | Scientific or reporting framework | ARRIVE guidelines |
| Scientific literature | Peer-reviewed research or review article | PubMed-indexed review |
| Experimental evidence | Data generated from an actual study | Published analytical or preclinical study |

### Important distinction

A regulatory guidance document explains regulatory or scientific
expectations.

A scientific review summarizes existing scientific knowledge.

An experimental study reports observations generated from research.

These source types should not be treated as interchangeable.

15. Regulatory Guidance vs Scientific Literature vs Experimental Evidence
Regulatory Guidance
Regulatory guidance explains how a regulatory authority or recognized
international organization approaches a scientific or regulatory issue.
Examples:
- FDA
- EMA
- WHO
- ICH
Scientific Literature
Scientific literature describes scientific knowledge, research findings,
reviews, and scientific discussions.
Examples:
- Peer-reviewed reviews
- Research articles
- PubMed-indexed publications
Experimental Evidence
Experimental evidence is the actual data generated from a study.
Examples:
- Analytical measurements
- Functional assay results
- PK measurements
- PD measurements
- Preclinical observations
Important distinction
A review article discussing an experiment is not the same thing as the
raw experimental data from that experiment.
A regulatory guideline describing analytical similarity is not itself
experimental evidence.

20. Important Scientific Caution
The existence of an authoritative source does not mean that every claim
made using that source is automatically correct.
Evidence should always be:
- Read in context
- Properly cited
- Compared with other relevant evidence
- Checked for limitations
- Reviewed by a qualified human when important decisions are involved
The project should avoid:
- Fabricating experimental results
- Presenting synthetic data as published evidence
- Treating a review as raw data
- Treating regulatory guidance as experimental evidence
- Making clinical decisions from the tracker
- Claiming regulatory approval
21. Synthetic Data Rule
The project includes a sample biosimilar evidence dataset for testing
the Python tracker.
Those records must be explicitly labeled:
SAMPLE / SYNTHETIC / FOR SOFTWARE TESTING
Synthetic records must never be presented as:
- Published experimental results
- Real clinical results
- Real animal-study results
- Regulatory evidence
- Actual biosimilar approval evidence

23. Known Limitations of This Registry
This registry is not an exhaustive bibliography of all biosimilarity
research.
Limitations include:
- Only selected authoritative sources are included.
- Scientific literature is continuously updated.
- Regulatory guidance can change.
- Different jurisdictions may use different requirements.
- Individual products can require product-specific evidence.
- A source may provide general principles rather than product-specific
  conclusions.
Future versions should update the registry when relevant guidance or
scientific evidence changes.

25. Final Source Registry Statement
The sources in this registry provide the scientific and regulatory
foundation for the Biosimilarity & Preclinical Evidence Quality
Foundation project.
The project uses these sources to:
- Learn biosimilarity concepts.
- Build an evidence map.
- Understand preclinical evidence.
- Develop a quality-control model.
- Organize sample evidence records.
- Build a basic evidence-tracking tool.
- Identify evidence gaps and limitations.
The registry does not itself establish biosimilarity, clinical safety,
clinical efficacy, or regulatory approval.
Scientific evidence must be interpreted in context and reviewed by
qualified human experts before being used for important scientific,
clinical, or regulatory decisions.
